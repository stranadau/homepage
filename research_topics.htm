Research Topics

  • (1) Modal behaviors of a Timoshenko beam with an edge crack
    We develop analytical / semi-analytical vibration models for cracked beam-like waveguides where shear deformation and rotary inertia are non-negligible. The emphasis is on interpretable frequency equations and coupling mechanisms (axial–bending and axial–bending–shear) that explain when and why modal sensitivity increases.
    • Deep-beam crack dynamics: for low aspect-ratio beams, mixed crack-mode effects can induce combined axial–bending–shear behavior near the crack, requiring coupled formulations rather than pure bending approximations.
    • Frequency-equation formulations: closed-form or reduced iterative equations are constructed to support rapid parametric studies and inversion-ready modeling under arbitrary support conditions.
    • High-frequency regime: we clarify limitations of “node-crack insensitivity” assumptions and quantify when higher modes provide stronger crack signatures.
    • Key papers: JSV 2024 (deep beam with transverse open crack), JSV 2020 (closed-form frequency equation with axial–bending coupling), Eng. Struct. 2018 (revisiting “higher-mode sensitivity” premise), IJMS 2022 (crack on a high-frequency bending node), Wave Motion 2017 (simplified frequency equation from a wave perspective), JSV 2022 (phase-closure based generic frequency equation)
  • (2) Physics-informed machine learning for engineering structures
    We design physics-informed learning workflows for time-dependent structural responses where governing equations are known but conventional calibration can be ill-posed under sparse/noisy measurements. The goal is data-efficient and physically consistent prediction/estimation suitable for engineering interpretation and decision-making.
    • PINN for time-dependent PSC behavior: physics constraints are embedded to learn coupled evolution of prestress and material/time-dependent mechanisms without relying solely on dense time discretization.
    • Engineering usability: emphasis on stable training, mechanistic interpretability, and model forms that can be integrated into SHM pipelines (state tracking and parameter inference).
    • Key papers: Sensors 2023 (PINN for early-age time-dependent behaviors of PSC beams), Structures 2024 (Volterra-integral-equation-based prestress-loss estimation)
  • (3) Guided wave propagation using surface-bonded piezoelectric wafers
    This theme studies wave-based interpretation and modeling of electromechanical signatures measured by surface-bonded PZT wafers. We connect impedance/admittance evolution to underlying wave fields, reflections, and damping—supporting sensing-range design and robust diagnostics.
    • Wave-to-signature linkage: analytical solutions (piezoelectricity + Timoshenko beam theory) are used to explain how repeated reflections and mode content shape measured admittance evolution.
    • Damping and sensing range: estimation of high-frequency damping using electromechanical signatures to improve practical assessment of detectability and effective sensing distance.
    • Extension to cement-based materials: multi-mode PZT sensing for in-situ profiling of evolving dynamic properties in cementitious systems.
    • Key papers: JIMSS 2014 (understanding electromechanical admittance from a wave perspective), JIMSS 2017 (evolution of electromechanical admittance on a Timoshenko beam), JKSCE 2017 (high-frequency damping via electromechanical signatures), Cem. Concr. Compos. 2025 (multi-mode PZT sensing for cement-based materials)
  • (4) Inverse problems and system identification
    Across our research lines, we target inverse estimation: inferring hidden structural states or parameters (e.g., crack severity, prestress loss, damping) from limited measurements. We focus on physically grounded formulations that improve identifiability and numerical stability in noise-sensitive settings.
    • Integral-equation view for estimation: Volterra-type formulations provide a physically interpretable route to estimate time-dependent prestress loss and its interaction with creep/shrinkage/relaxation mechanisms.
    • Inversion-ready reduced models: frequency-equation and wave-based reduced models enable rapid forward evaluations that are practical for optimization, calibration, and crack assessment.
    • Key papers: Structures 2024 (Volterra integral equation → finite difference estimation), Wave Motion 2017 (wave-based simplified frequency equation), JSV 2022 (generic frequency equation via phase closure)
