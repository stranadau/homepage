Modal behaviors of a Timoshenko beam with an edge crack

We develop analytical and semi-analytical formulations to quantify crack-induced changes in modal characteristics of beam-like waveguides, particularly when shear deformation and rotary inertia require a Timoshenko-type description. A recurring emphasis is mechanistic interpretability: explicit frequency equations and coupling mechanisms that explain when and why modal sensitivity increases, supporting both forward prediction and inversion-ready modeling.

Physics-informed machine learning for engineering structures

We develop physics-informed learning pipelines for structural mechanics problems where governing equations are known but parameter estimation and prediction can be ill-posed under sparse/noisy measurements. The focus is on embedding physics (constraints from mechanics and evolution laws) into learning to obtain data-efficient, physically consistent, and engineering-usable models for prediction and state/parameter inference.

Guided wave propagation using surface-bonded piezoelectric wafers

This theme investigates guided-wave excitation, propagation, scattering, and wavefield manipulation using surface-bonded piezoelectric (PZT) wafers/transducers for active SHM. We emphasize physics-based interpretation of wave/electromechanical signals, time-reversal acoustics, baseline-independent diagnostics, and efficient coupled PZT–structure modeling.

Inverse problems and system identification

We develop numerically stable inverse-analysis and system-identification methodologies to infer hidden structural states and parameters from limited, noise-contaminated measurements. Emphasis is placed on regularization design (Tikhonov, sparsity-promoting, and spatial regularization), robust hyperparameter selection, and practical inverse pipelines such as acceleration-to-displacement reconstruction for low-frequency dominant structures.
